# ScamDrill — Real-User Test Kit

A short, respectful way to try ScamDrill with one elderly family member and write down what happened. It takes about 15 minutes in total. You run it yourself, in person, with the person's consent.

## Before you start

- **Choose the person thoughtfully.** Pick someone who is comfortable with a phone and in good spirits that day. **Don't** test with anyone who has memory or cognitive difficulties, who is unwell, or who has recently been scammed or frightened by a call. For them, even a practice drill could be upsetting.
- **Pick a calm time and place,** and sit next to them. Nobody should be rushed.
- **Use the app on your own phone or laptop,** in the language they're most comfortable with (English, हिन्दी or Hinglish). Fill in Setup yourself with first names only.
- **Don't use real details** beyond first names and the bank's name. The drill's OTP and account number are fake.
- **Decide the recording question in advance.** You may record *only* if they agree out loud, and only a few seconds of their reaction or voice. Never record their face or screen without a separate "yes".

## Consent script (read aloud, in your own words is fine)

> "I've made a small practice app to help people spot phone scams, like the 'digital arrest' calls where someone pretends to be from the CBI or police. I'd like you to try it for a few minutes.
>
> **It is completely fake.** Nobody real will call or message you. It's just a chat on this phone, with a pretend officer. The code and the bank account in it are made up. Nothing you do can move money or share anything.
>
> The pretend officer will try to pressure you, the way real scammers do. Your job is to notice the tricks and, whenever you want, press **Hang up** or **Call** me. There's no wrong answer, and it's not a test of you. It's a test of my app.
>
> **You can stop at any time, for any reason,** and that's completely fine.
>
> Afterwards I'd like to ask you two or three questions. With your permission, I might record a few seconds of your voice or reaction to use in a short video about the app. I'll only do that if you say yes, and I won't use your face or full name. Is that okay?
>
> Would you like to try it?"

Record their answers under **Results** below: yes or no to the drill, and yes or no to the recording, separately.

## During the drill: observer checklist

Stay quiet during the drill. Don't prompt them or help them. If they look upset or confused, stop the drill and reassure them.

| What to note | Your notes |
|---|---|
| Language chosen (English / हिन्दी / Hinglish) | |
| Did they read the "training drill" strip and the handoff message? | |
| **When did they end the call?** (message number, or "didn't") | |
| **How did it end?** Hang up / Call family / typed the OTP / tapped Pay / tried to share another number / stayed to the end | |
| **Which tactic seemed to work on them?** (the chip on screen at the moment they hesitated or gave in) | |
| Did they notice the red-flag chips or the pressure meter? | |
| Anything they said out loud during the drill (quote, if possible) | |
| Anything confusing about the screen (text size, buttons, language) | |

## Afterwards: three questions

1. "How did that feel?"
2. "At what point did you think something was wrong?"
3. "If a call like this came tomorrow, what would you do?"

Then **show them the report card together,** and read the "Practise next time" tip aloud. End on the real-life step: *hang up, call **1930**, or report at **cybercrime.gov.in**.*

## Debrief (always)

- Remind them clearly that **it was fake** and nothing happened to any account.
- If they "lost", say what the app says: *"This is how it happens to careful people."* Practising is exactly what makes the difference.
- Ask whether they'd like to try again another day.
